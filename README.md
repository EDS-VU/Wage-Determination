This study explores the factors influencing wages, focusing on whether parental education plays a dominant role or if other variables (such as individual education, IQ, work experience, and personal background) are stronger predictors.

Using a dataset of 935 individuals, we tested three modeling approaches:

Linear Regression

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)

Regression Trees

Key findings:

Parental education has a significant impact, but its effect diminishes when individual education and IQ are included.

Other factors such as work experience, marital status, and urban residency further improve wage predictions.

Linear regression produced the most accurate predictions (lowest MSE), outperforming KNN and regression trees.

A one-year increase in parental education is associated with approximately 1.8% higher wages.

A simulation study confirmed that linear regression remains reliable even when residuals are not perfectly normal, reinforcing its effectiveness in this context.
